## 1. Selected Historical Settings and Justification

The original Project Victoria architecture established a dual-port British maritime infrastructure:
1. **Port A — Port of Aldermouth (Passenger & Liner Terminal):**
   - **Historical Archetype:** The Port of Liverpool and the Mersey Docks & Harbour Board estate c. 1889.
   - **Justification:** *SS Aurelian* (the ocean liner developed under `agent/ship` by Leonidas-II) is documented as operating for the "Albion & Western Steam Navigation Co., Liverpool" on the premier Liverpool – Queenstown – New York transatlantic route. Liverpool in 1889 was the undisputed capital of the Atlantic steam trade, handling over a third of Britain's foreign trade. Its tidal range of over 30 feet necessitated two world-famous engineering solutions:
     1. The **Floating Landing Stage** (Prince's and George's Landing Stages), moored on iron pontoons and connected to the shore by long articulated bridges, allowing 160-metre liners to embark passengers regardless of the tide.
     2. An **enclosed non-tidal wet dock system** protected by half-tide basins and lock gates, where cargo vessels and colliers discharged in still water.
   - **Supplementary Reference:** Bristol Floating Harbour (Historic England JRC01/01/001/001 & JRC01/01/031/009; Jessop's 1809 impoundment of the River Avon), demonstrating the engineering of impounded commercial basins, battered Pennant stone quay walls, heavy granite coping stones, cast-iron mooring rings, and creosoted oak fender piling.

2. **Port B — Port of Carrickmore (Shipbuilding & Heavy Marine Engineering):**
   - **Historical Archetype:** Queen's Island shipyard, River Lagan, Belfast (Harland & Wolff and Belfast Harbour Commissioners) c. 1889.
   - **Justification:** In 1889, Belfast was the premier shipyard centre of the British Empire, having launched the *Teutonic* (yard no. 214, launched January 1889, completed August 1889), the world's first armed merchant cruiser and fastest transatlantic liner of the era.
   - **Principal Works Reconstructed:**
     1. **Alexandra Graving Dock (modelled as Princess Graving Dock):** Begun 1885, completed and opened in 1889. Dimensions: 800 ft (243.8 m) length, 80 ft (24.4 m) clear width, 31 ft (9.4 m) depth. Designed by W.R. Kelly, Belfast Harbour Engineer. It was the largest dry dock in the world upon opening and featured two intermediate caisson grooves allowing division into three independent dry docks or stepped flooding.
     2. **Building Slipways (Slips 1–3):** Timber declivity ways (inclined 1 in 16) equipped with heavy pitch-pine launch ways, sliding ways, oak keel blocks, and bilge shoring. Sized to late-1880s proportions (prior to the 1908 Olympic-class reconstruction).
     3. **Masting & Fitting-Out Gear:** Replacement of the anachronistic 1907 Titan crane with authentic **80-ton Day Summers / Armstrong tubular wrought-iron sheer legs** placed over Berth 1, with front legs raked over the basin and a telescopic back-stay worked by an engine-driven horizontal screw.
     4. **The Moulding Loft & Engine Works:** A 50-metre top-lit scriving loft floor where naval architects laid out lines full-size, directly adjacent to the boiler shop, smithy, and steam sawmill.

## 2. Primary Source Register

### 2.1 Archival Photographs & Historic Collections
1. **Historic England Archive — Janette Rosing Collection (Bristol Floating Harbour):**
   - *Item JRC01/01/001/001 (1870–1900):* Broad Quay looking east towards multi-storey brick and stone bonded warehouses, drawbridge connecting Broad Quay with St Augustine's Parade, multiple sailing vessels and coastal steamers moored at stone quayside.
   - *Item JRC01/01/031/009 (1860–1880):* Broad Quay in St Augustine's Reach, showing multi-course battered stone quay walls, heavy timber rubbing strakes, dockers and seamen at work, cargo crates and barrels staged on the quay edge.
2. **London Museum — Port and River Archive:**
   - Engineering plans and photographic survey of West India Docks (No. 1 Warehouse, built 1802, Jesse Hartley and George Gwilt brick architecture; brick jack arches, cast-iron columns, timber beam floors, iron cathead hoists, multi-pane sash windows with segmental brick arches).
   - Port of London Authority collection: Armstrong hydraulic dock machinery, hydraulic jigger cylinders, accumulator towers, and quayside portal cranes.
3. **Bristol Archives — Maritime Records:**
   - *Bristol's Maritime Records (Selected Sources Guide):* Harbour Master records, dock engineering drawings, swing bridge mechanisms, dock gates, and lock sluice culverts.

### 2.2 Contemporary Technical Literature (c. 1880–1892)
1. **Chadwick, F.E., Kelly, J.D.J., Rideing, W.H., et al. (1891):**
   - *Ocean Steamships: A Popular Account of their Construction, Development, Management and Appliances.* New York: Charles Scribner's Sons / Project Gutenberg eBook #54136.
   - *Plate "Landing Stages at Liverpool":* Detailed albumen engraving of George's Landing Stage, showing tubular iron pontoons, articulated truss gangway bridges, passenger ticket gates, and tender steamers.
   - *Plates on Coaling and Cargo:* Coaling from offside canal barges using steam hoists; package cargo handling with 6-gang winch teams; live cattle holding pens on quay aprons.
2. **Vernon-Harcourt, Leveson Francis (1885):**
   - *Harbours and Docks: Their Physical Features, History, Construction, Equipment, and Maintenance.* Oxford: Clarendon Press.
   - Vol. I (Text) & Vol. II (Plates): Measured cross-sections of Hartley's cyclopean granite quay walls at Liverpool; hydraulic lock machinery; Alexandra Dock (Belfast) cross-sections and caisson seatings; graving dock timber shoring and bilge blocks.
3. **Cunningham, Brysson (1904 / referencing 1880s practice):**
   - *The Principles and Practice of Dock Engineering.* London: Charles Griffin & Co.
   - Detailed structural mechanics of masonry quay wall batter (typically 1:12 to 1:24), granite coping arrises, recessed cast-iron ladders, mooring bollard anchoring into massive concrete footings, and hydraulic swing bridge turntables.
4. **Armstrong, Sir William (1877/1888):**
   - *The Application of Water Pressure to Dock and Harbour Machinery.* Proceedings of the Institution of Civil Engineers.
   - Design of hydraulic portal cranes straddling standard-gauge railway tracks; accumulator towers with weighted 700 psi iron rams; hydraulic capstans with three-cylinder oscillating hydraulic engines.

## 3. Strict 1889 Historical Cut-off Register

To ensure total authenticity, the following post-1889 technologies and landmarks are **strictly excluded**:

| Technology / Work | Historical Date | Why Excluded | 1889 Authenticity Equivalent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Giant Cantilever Titan Crane** | 1904–1914 | Edwardian electric shipyard crane; non-existent in 1889. | **80-ton tubular masting sheer legs** (Armstrong / Day Summers) with steam screw back-stay. |
| **Arrol Portal Gantry** | 1905–1908 | Built over Harland & Wolff slips 2 and 3 for the Olympic class. | **Timber building slips with mobile yard steam cranes and derrick poles**. |
| **Thompson Graving Dock** | 1902–1911 | Titanic dry dock; post-dates 1889 horizon. | **Princess (Alexandra) Graving Dock (1885–1889)**, 800 × 80 ft. |
| **Electric Dock Cranes** | 1898+ | Electric motor drives on dock quays post-date 1889. | **Armstrong hydraulic portal cranes** and **vertical-boiler steam travelling cranes**. |
| **Electric Quayside Lighting (General)** | 1893+ | Extensive quayside electric incandescent grid was rare before the 1890s. | **Victorian cast-iron gas streetlamps** with hexagonal glass lanterns and oil lanterns on pontoons. |
| **Steel Container / Monorail Systems** | 20th Century | Unhistorical modern cargo systems. | **Standard-gauge dock railways**, wooden 7-plank coal wagons, sack barrows, horse drays, hand trollies. |
